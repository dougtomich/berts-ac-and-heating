Why Your AC Isn't Cooling in Cypress, TX

November 23, 2026

When summer temperatures in Cypress, Texas push past 95°F, a struggling air conditioner is more than an inconvenience — it's an emergency. If your AC is running but your home still feels warm, you're not alone. Homeowners across Cy-Fair, Bridgeland, Towne Lake, and Fairfield deal with this every year. Here are the most common reasons your AC isn't cooling and what you can do about it.

1. Dirty or Clogged Air Filter

This is the number one culprit. Houston-area humidity means your filter collects moisture and dust faster than you'd expect. A clogged filter restricts airflow over the evaporator coil, causing the system to work harder and cool less. Fix: Check your filter monthly during summer. If it looks gray or matted, replace it. Standard 1-inch filters should be swapped every 30 days when the system runs daily.

2. Frozen Evaporator Coil

Ironically, a frozen coil means no cold air. Low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or blocked return vents can drop coil temperatures below freezing, forming ice that blocks airflow entirely. If you see frost on the indoor unit or copper lines, turn the system to "fan only" for a few hours to thaw, then call a technician.

3. Refrigerant Leak

Your AC doesn't consume refrigerant — it circulates it in a closed loop. If your system is low on refrigerant, there's a leak. Signs include:

  • Warm air from the vents even with the thermostat set low
  • Ice buildup on the outdoor unit or refrigerant lines
  • A hissing or bubbling sound near the indoor unit

Refrigerant leaks require a licensed HVAC technician to locate, repair, and recharge. This is not a DIY fix, and in Texas the refrigerant itself is regulated under EPA Section 608.

4. Thermostat Issues

Before assuming the worst, check the basics. Make sure your thermostat is set to "cool" (not "heat" or "fan only"), and that the temperature is set below the current room temperature. Dead batteries in wireless thermostats are surprisingly common. If you have an older mercury thermostat, it may be time for a programmable or smart upgrade that keeps your home cooler while you're away.

5. Condenser Coil Blocked by Debris

The outdoor unit needs clear airflow to dump heat. In Cypress neighborhoods like Bridgeland and Towne Lake where landscaping is a point of pride, it's common for shrubs, mulch, grass clippings, and cottonwood fluff to block the condenser fins. Keep at least two feet of clearance around the unit and hose down the fins gently once a season.

6. Undersized System for Your Home

Many newer homes in the Cy-Fair area are 2,500 to 4,000 square feet with high ceilings and large windows. If your AC was sized for minimum code rather than actual cooling load, it will struggle on the hottest days. A proper Manual J load calculation — not a rule-of-thumb estimate — tells you whether your system is matched to your home.

7. Ductwork Leaks

In Houston's attic heat, even small duct leaks waste a huge amount of cooled air. The Department of Energy estimates that typical duct systems lose 20 to 30 percent of conditioned air through leaks and poor connections. If some rooms cool fine and others stay warm, leaky or disconnected ducts are a likely cause.

When to Call a Professional

Start with the easy checks: filter, thermostat, and outdoor unit clearance. If those look fine and your home still isn't cooling, it's time to call a licensed technician. Electrical components, refrigerant, and ductwork repairs require professional tools and training.
