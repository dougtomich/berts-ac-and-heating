5 Ways to Lower Your Energy Bills This Summer in Cypress, TX

November 23, 2026

Houston-area summers are expensive. Electricity bills for a typical Cypress home can spike to $300 or more per month when your air conditioner runs 12+ hours a day from June through September. The good news: most of the biggest savings come from HVAC maintenance and smart habits — not major equipment upgrades. Here are five practical ways to cut your cooling costs this summer.

1. Set Your Thermostat to 78°F When You're Home

The Department of Energy recommends 78°F as the balance point between comfort and efficiency. Every degree below 78 adds roughly 3 to 5 percent to your cooling costs. That means keeping your thermostat at 72°F instead of 78°F could cost you 18 to 30 percent more per month.

A programmable or smart thermostat makes this painless. Set it to 78°F while you're home, 85°F while you're at work, and let it pre-cool the house 30 minutes before you return. If you work from home — common in Cypress's newer communities like Bridgeland and Towne Lake — ceiling fans let you raise the thermostat 4°F without feeling warmer.

2. Change Your Air Filter Every 30 Days

This is the single cheapest thing you can do. A dirty filter forces your blower motor to work harder, drawing more electricity and shortening the life of the motor. In Houston's humid, dusty air, a standard 1-inch pleated filter gets clogged faster than the 90-day interval printed on the packaging.

Pro tip: Buy a 12-pack of filters at the start of summer and set a monthly phone reminder. A $5 filter swap can save $20 or more in electricity each month.

3. Seal and Insulate Your Ductwork

Your attic can reach 140°F on a July afternoon in Cypress. If your ducts run through that attic — and in most Texas homes they do — every leak and gap in the ductwork dumps cooled air straight into that oven. The Department of Energy estimates duct losses account for 20 to 30 percent of conditioned air in a typical system.

Signs your ducts need attention:

  • Some rooms are noticeably warmer than others
  • Your energy bills are higher than your neighbors' with a similar-sized home
  • Visible gaps or disconnected sections at duct joints in the attic

A professional duct sealing and insulation job typically pays for itself within one to two cooling seasons in the Houston area.

4. Keep the Outdoor Unit Clean and Clear

Your condenser (the big outdoor box) has to dump all the heat your indoor unit absorbs. If the condenser coils are coated in dirt, pollen, or cottonwood fuzz, it can't reject heat efficiently, so the compressor runs longer and harder.

Once a month during summer:

  1. Turn off the system at the thermostat
  2. Gently hose down the condenser fins from the inside out
  3. Clear any leaves, mulch, or grass clippings within two feet of the unit
  4. Make sure the pad or slab is level — settling happens in Houston clay soil

5. Schedule a Pre-Summer Tune-Up

An annual maintenance visit catches small problems before they become expensive repairs. A licensed HVAC technician will check refrigerant levels, clean the evaporator coil, test electrical connections, and verify the system is running at peak efficiency.

Combined, these five steps can realistically save a Cypress homeowner $75 to $150+ per month during peak summer. More importantly, they extend the life of your equipment and reduce the chance of a breakdown on the hottest day of the year.

The Bottom Line

You don't need a new system to cut your energy bills. Start with a clean filter, a reasonable thermostat setting, and a quick inspection of your outdoor unit. For everything else — duct sealing, tune-ups, and refrigerant checks — a local HVAC professional can get your system running at its best before the heat arrives.
